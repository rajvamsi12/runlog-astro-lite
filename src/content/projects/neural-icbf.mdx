## The problem

A control barrier function (CBF) is a way to prove a robot stays safe: it defines a safe region and blocks any action that would leave it. The catch is that it needs the robot's exact equations of motion. Real systems rarely come with those.

## The idea

Learn every missing piece from data, then keep the one part that gives the guarantee - a small optimization that minimally corrects the controller's action - exactly as it is.

## How it works

The filter runs once per control step:

1. **Learned dynamics.** A Neural ODE, trained on recorded trajectories, predicts how the system moves.
2. **Learned controller.** A policy imitates an expensive optimal controller (NMPC) so it runs fast.
3. **Learned barrier.** A network trained on safe and unsafe examples scores how safe a state is.
4. **Safety filter.** A quadratic program (QP) picks the action closest to the controller's that keeps the barrier positive and respects the car's limits.

## The insight: labels have to look ahead

To train the barrier you label states as safe or unsafe. My first labels just checked whether a state was inside the safe region. That was wrong: some states are inside it but already doomed, because no allowed action can stop the system from leaving.

I changed the labels to check whether *some* allowed action keeps the system safe over time. This relabeled 28.95% of the training data as unsafe and brought input-constraint violations in closed loop from 0.80% to 0.00%.

## Racing

Then I pushed it to something harder: a 6-dimensional model of an F1TENTH car with tire grip limits, steering rate limits and motor lag, racing a 50 m track full of obstacles. The learned dynamics predict 1 second ahead with a median error of 8.5 cm.

Results on 200 obstacle layouts never used during development, at 4.0 m/s:

| Method | Success | Unsafe (crash or off track) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planner alone (MPPI) | 84.0% | 12 of 200 |
| Analytical HOCBF-QP | 27.5% | 70 of 200 |
| **Neural ICBF + planner** | **84.5%** | **0 of 200** |

The gain is in safety, not success: the success rate is about the same as the planner alone, and most remaining failures are the car stopping in front of an obstacle, not crashing.

## Where it breaks, and the fix

Near the grip limit the front tires saturate, and steering stops working the way a one-step filter assumes - it can even make things worse. I found this failure and built a sampling-based predictive filter that looks further ahead. At 6 m/s it reaches 90-93% success with zero crashes.

## Paper

This work is part of a co-authored ACC paper on neural integral control barrier functions for unknown nonlinear systems with input constraints. I also derived a closed-form bound showing the integral control law tracks the learned policy.
