Control barrier functions give you a safety guarantee - if you know the dynamics. This project, at the University of Virginia, asks what happens when you don't.

Code: [github.com/rajvamsi12/agile-nicbf](https://github.com/rajvamsi12/agile-nicbf)

## The pipeline

Every piece is learned except the final optimization:

- **Dynamics** - a Neural ODE trained on trajectories.
- **Policy** - imitation-learned from an NMPC expert.
- **Barrier** - a network trained on labeled safe and unsafe states, with Lie derivatives computed by autodiff.
- **Filter** - a QP that stays as close as possible to the policy while respecting the barrier and input constraints.

I also derived a closed-form exponential bound on the tracking error, showing that the integral control law tracks the imitation-learned policy.

## Labels have to look ahead

Geometric labels were not enough. A state can sit inside the safe region and still be doomed, because no admissible input can keep it there. Forward-invariance-aware rollout labeling relabeled **28.95%** of training samples as unsafe and took closed-loop input-constraint violations from **0.80% to 0.00%**.

## Racing

I extended the method to a 6-D dynamic bicycle model of an F1TENTH car with tire-force saturation, steering slew limits and motor lag.

| | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Neural ODE median 1 s rollout error | 8.5 cm |
| QP filter time per step (one CPU core) | 7-8 ms |
| Crashes + off-track, held-out layouts @ 4.0 m/s | 12/200 -> 0/200 (p = 0.0005) |
| Success rate kept | ~84% |
| Analytical HOCBF-QP baseline failures | 29-35% |

Barrier networks were labeled with a 22-maneuver escape-set analysis.

## Where one-step filters fail

Near the grip limit, front-tire saturation reverses the steering response the QP assumes, so a one-step barrier filter can make things worse. A sampling-based predictive safety filter fixes this, reaching **90-93% success with zero crashes at 6 m/s**.

This work fed into an ACC paper on Neural ICBFs for unknown nonlinear systems with input constraints.
